Worldwide water need: coverage and sources

Expanded 2026-09-04. 78 new reported crisis locations across 26 countries join 156 existing community records, for 234 world-need records. National context is separate: 217 World Bank countries/economies, 205 with at-least-basic estimates and 150 with safely-managed estimates. These are worldwide reference datasets, not a complete inventory of every locality or active advisory.

How to read the map

Amber markers are documented communities or city/region reports. New crisis cards show the source period and spatial scope. Reports describe conditions at the time; humanitarian response or restoration does not prove a crisis is still active. Coordinates are rounded place reference points, not household or facility locations, boundaries or service areas. Older community records are retained and were not all independently revalidated in this expansion.

Country context markers are outlined violet/grey circles located at the World Bank capital/reference coordinate. They do not identify a shortage in that capital. Six records without coordinates remain in search and the Countries list. National percentages never receive a DEWFALL yield or fit score. Unknown values are not zero. Overlapping national, regional and community figures must not be added together.

National data

WHO/UNICEF JMP: at least basic drinking water, via World Bank · Safely managed drinking water · JMP definitions and household data. Import uses the newest non-null observation from 2020–2025 for each metric separately; most observations are 2024. At least basic means an improved source within a 30-minute round trip. Safely managed requires an improved source on premises, available when needed and free from priority contamination. Missing safely-managed data cannot be inferred from basic access.

Excluded and remaining gaps

Brixham’s 2024 boil-water incident was not added as an active need: the utility reports it was lifted. Catalonia’s past drought was not converted into an active local advisory. Coverage remains uneven below country level; many local advisories have no unified global feed. More site-level records need official locality evidence, geocoding and recurring status checks.
